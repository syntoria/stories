Syntoria case study

Unanet

Leadership development for the senior leadership team · 2025–2026

Part I of VII

Before the program

1The company and the buyer

Unanet sells project-based ERP and CRM software to government contractors and to architecture, engineering and construction firms — more than 4,000 client organizations in 2024, backed by the private-equity firm JMI Equity since 2019, growing fast and run at a rigorous pace by an executive team of about ten.

Unanet company materials and public record, 2019–2024

Most of what follows rests on one witness: Stacy Critzer, Unanet's Chief Human Resources Officer — over 25 years of HR leadership, SVP of Human Resources at Inovalon and Avalere before this role. She leads the senior leadership team the program set out to develop and saw these leaders work week after week. She commissioned the program, followed it through, and assessed the result.

Unanet leadership page · public record

2What the executives saw

The executives were watching their senior layer — vice presidents, senior vice presidents, a few directors — wait for permission: decisions went up to the top that these leaders could have made themselves. The cost was pace.

“And the funny thing was no one ever told them they couldn't make the decision. But for some reason, they did not possess the confidence or think they had the authority or the autonomy to do it.”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

Left alone, the problem had a price: thin bench strength, a succession plan that “would not have succeeded”, and, in her projection, lost talent.

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

3The decision

By 2024 the executive team had reached the limit of what it could carry. The conclusion was to build a stronger next layer of leadership.

“we recognized pretty early on like we can't continue to carry everything. We're going to have to leverage this next layer of leadership.”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

Part II of VII

The engagement

4Why him

The executives had committed to developing this layer when they created it; delivery went outside for capacity — 25 to 30 people against a small HR team focused on the rest of the organization.

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

The person they brought in was Josh Dietrich, of the firm Arete Pursuits. He was not a new supplier: Unanet had first hired him in 2022 for a one-hour training, then for a string of short engagements — goals, feedback, on-site team facilitation, a DiSC session — each larger than the last, until the mid-five-figure program this story tells.

Josh Dietrich · statements of work, 2022–2024

He had entered the shortlist by internal referral, weighed against bigger vendors the company had also worked with. The prior engagements drove the choice.

“we had done some work with Josh previously, so there was success there that we could point to”

“it's kind of a no-brainer.”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

Behind the history sits an operator's career — three decades in leadership, an SVP role over a global organization of more than 500 people, nearly 2,000 coaching hours — with ICF Professional Certified Coach, NeuroTransformational coaching and Leadership Circle Profile certifications.

Josh Dietrich · coaching bio, 2026

The neuroscience grounding is also how the buyer describes him.

“He's just authentic… He's real… He approaches his coaching from a neuroscience perspective, which I think is incredibly helpful for people. And he individualizes it.”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

5The contract

What caught her interest was the Leadership Circle Profile — the 360 assessment he was certified on — offered on its own, unbundled from a coaching package. She asked him for a proposal to run it for every member of the senior leadership team, the same process for all.

Josh Dietrich · coach interview, 11 August 2026

The contract bound the diagnosis to the teaching.

Statement of work 0001-009 · effective 30 August 2024

“Contractor will deliver four 90-minute leadership workshops throughout the year. The content will be determined based on themes identified from the LCP. Sponsor and Coach will mutually agree on workshop topics.”

Statement of work 0001-009 · signed 1 October 2024

One clause keeps each leader's results private — even from her: each leader has sole discretion to share their assessment or not.

Statement of work 0001-009 · signed 1 October 2024

She had defined success before the work began: the executives would be able to hand a leader a real piece of work and see it carried from start to finish. And she set a number, hedged as a hope — “call it a quarter of them” standing out.

“you own this and go build a plan… come back to me as the executive, and we would be able to see them actually take something from the beginning to the end. And that to us was success”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

Part III of VII

The work

6How Josh worked

The work began in January 2025: first the 360 assessments of the senior leadership team — 31 leaders debriefed in the first year — then the workshops, and, running continuously alongside, the coaching: 132 hours across 33 leaders, about four hours per leader.

Josh Dietrich · coaching ledger

His way of working pairs two things. The first is presence: the individualized, one-on-one attention the buyer describes. The second is a methodical layer underneath — the 360s, and a tracker he updated after every debrief, ticking off each strength and each declared area of work per leader, so that what fed the next step was what the data said rather than his read of the room.

Josh Dietrich · coach interview, 11 August 2026

Working across the whole layer let him connect patterns across dozens of leaders while holding each leader's results confidential.

Josh Dietrich · coach interview, 11 August 2026

7The assessments and what they found

Every 360 ran under rules written into the contract: a self-assessment, evaluators in five categories, a category reported only once five people respond, anonymous comments, two debrief sessions. 30 of 32 leaders completed both.

Statement of work 0001-009 · Josh Dietrich's coaching ledger

In each leader's debrief he noted the themes that leader wanted — or needed — to work on, and counted them across the layer. The result is a ranked diagnosis: delegation led, raised by 19 of the 27 leaders assessed (70%), ahead of strategic thinking (48%), balance and stress (41%), and a passive pattern paired with low courageous authenticity (41%).

Josh Dietrich · per-leader theme tracker

The top theme is a finding of its own. The Leadership Circle Profile has its own fixed dimensions, and delegation is not one of them — the instrument's scoring could never have surfaced it. It came out of the debrief conversations: leader after leader named the same gap, and the tracker counted it.

Theme tracker, read against the instrument's dimension list

He also tested correlations across the findings and reported them in the April 2025 sponsor debrief deck — the buyer saw the analysis, not only the themes, including a result that came back null.

Sponsor debrief deck · April 2025 · p.20

“Correlation between Communication and Passive/Courageous Authenticity — No correlation. Only one leader had both themes.”

Sponsor debrief deck · April 2025

8The workshops and the coaching

The workshops taught what the diagnosis found. The format was four 90-minute workshops, their topics set by the assessment themes; he delivered three, and the fourth became the program's closing capstone session, in October.

Statement of work · written answers, August 2026

Effective delegation (April 2025) answered the top theme — the one deck built from scratch for this client. The other two, on strategic thinking and stress management, are licensed and disclosed as such; the customisation sat in the diagnosis and the selection of topics.

Workshop decks 1–3 · 2025

Even the in-room exercises protected confidentiality: practice requests had to be unrelated to the group. Many participants cited the delegation and strategic-thinking workshops as impactful.

Workshop 1 deck · Josh Dietrich's written preparation form

The coaching worked to goals set with each leader — and they were cleared by session six of twelve, against his stated norm that goals span the full engagement. The one-on-one breadth also made him useful on hiring: he helped guide two executive hires, giving the client an independent read drawn from what the leaders told him.

Josh Dietrich · coach interview, 11 August 2026

Part IV of VII

The results

9What changed

The bar Stacy Critzer set at purchase was cleared: after the program, she reported growth “even beyond just a quarter of them”.

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

And the change she reports lands where the diagnosis pointed. The April 2025 sponsor deck's discussion agenda had put clarity of company strategy — and cascading it down the organization — on the table; sixteen months later, the change she names first is on the same ground.

Sponsor debrief deck · April 2025

“…confidence from the senior leaders and their ability to translate a business problem into a strategy that they could then execute with their teams. That was the most important thing that we saw coming out of it.”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

It shows in behavior she can point to.

“I can tap somebody on a shoulder and say can you present at this next upcoming all employee meeting on X, Y and Z and they're like sure no problem — whereas before it would have been like can you ask somebody else like I don't know.”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

Day to day, leaders now reach out to their executives asking what else they can take on, and she has seen them present at board level during quarterly business reviews.

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

On the executive side she reports more leverage of the layer. And when something goes off the rails, the executives point leaders back to what they learned in the program — the corrective conversation now happens without the coach in it.

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

10Two leaders

One arc runs complete, from before to business result: a vice president the company already saw as high-potential, with a tendency to move fast, who before the program would “leave everybody smoking in the background of her wake”. After it, by the buyer's account, she asked the right questions, ran meetings her boss used to run, and in the current talent review stands ready for the next role — backed far enough that the company has put her through a program run by its investor, JMI. She also extended her own coaching, at her own initiative. Stacy splits the credit between the program and the leader's own appetite to learn.

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

Her unit's result is in the buyer's writing.

“…were able to meet or exceed their utilization targets as a result of that leader's focus on strategy, clear expectations and communication with her team. Prior to that they were missing their targets.”

Stacy Critzer · in writing, 13 August 2026

A second leader ran business development as a manager — managing the work without setting vision or owning the metrics. After the program, by her account, he decided the org structure, set the expectations and the metrics the team needed to hit — and the team began to flourish.

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

11The promotions

Across the whole layer, the outcome shows in promotions.

“Out of 26 senior leaders, 12 were promoted over the last 3 years with a direct correlation to Josh's leadership program and coaching.”

Stacy Critzer · in writing, 13 August 2026

The layer that was coached is now coaching: she sees these leaders building and developing their own teams.

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

12The capstone

The program closed on a capstone.

“the most defining moment of the program was at the capstone at the end where they all had to present back to the executive team, their learnings, their experience and what they were carrying forward”

“they had the confidence to stand up… and there's no way for me to put a measure on that”

“there's nothing better than that to see that happening and we… we've been able to see it actually… play out in the business”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

Part V of VII

What didn't work

13What the buyer would change

Of the program's two components, she would protect the coaching.

“I wouldn't reduce the coaching because I think that's incredibly valuable. I might have carved back some of the group work”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

The attendance record: 19, 10 and 16 leaders across the three workshops, and three leaders attended all of them.

Josh Dietrich · coaching ledger

One coach did not fit everyone — and she had priced that in at purchase: a good match for the majority, with some leaders needing more than what was offered.

“there may have been people who went through the program and got value out of it but did not continue… maybe it wasn't the best fit”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

14What the record cannot show

The 360s were run once per leader. A second round, proposed in writing for 12 to 18 months out, would have measured each leader's precise progress; it was not funded.

Sponsor debrief deck, April 2025 · written answers, August 2026

The program's own design also limited what the executives could see. Each leader's results and progress were confidential — so what was working stayed largely inside the coaching relationships, and reached the sponsors through behavior rather than reports. The impact was real to the leaders before it was visible to the buyers, and it was never systematically collected upward.

“Because of the confidentiality, ELT members didn't get as much concrete feedback on what made an impact.”

Josh Dietrich · written preparation form, 2026

Part VI of VII

Since then

15Her verdict, and since

On a seven-point scale, her rating of the change was the top option — much better. Her verdict on the engagement keeps its own limit.

“I would say it met what I expected… I think it was a newer engagement for Josh, meaning at this scale and size of what was being asked of him… he met the expectations, he wasn't at that point in time exceeding it.”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

The clearest signal since came from the leaders themselves.

“I had a lot of people that came back to me after the program ended and said can I continue”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

The engagement itself changed shape: from a diagnostic year — most of 2025's hours were 360 debriefs — to a standing per-leader practice, with coaching at 86% of 2026's hours and a fresh statement of work drawn for each new leader the client brings him. The hours are fewer, and they are per-leader now.

Josh Dietrich · coaching ledger and written answers, August 2026

In April 2026 she signed a letter of recommendation for him — for another company's procurement, covering the three-year relationship — and ticked the box.

Letter of recommendation · 7 April 2026 · §6

“Would you recommend this coach? Yes ☒ No ☐ — If yes, for what types of engagements: Coaching, facilitation, customization of content and delivery.”

Stacy Critzer · letter of recommendation, 7 April 2026

Part VII of VII

Where it stands

16Today

They started the year waiting to be told what to do, and ended it standing in front of the executive team explaining what they needed from them.

What the buyer wants now is to keep developing the team — leaders closing the gaps they still see in themselves, and getting ready for the next role.

“I would want them to continue to address those gaps that they see in themselves or where they think they're not quite ready for the next thing”

Stacy Critzer · client interview, 12 August 2026

How this case study was built

Note on sources and method

Sources. One recorded interview with the buyer, Unanet's Chief Human Resources Officer, 12 August 2026. Two interviews and a set of written answers from the coach, Josh Dietrich, August 2026. The engagement's own documents: the statement of work, the three workshop decks, the April 2025 sponsor debrief deck, the per-leader theme tracker, the coaching ledger, and a letter of recommendation dated 7 April 2026.

Procedure. Claims were extracted from each source independently, graded on provenance, and cross-checked against the documents before selection. Where a spoken figure and a document disagree, the document governs. Figures are reported with the denominator and time window they were measured on; cohort counts differ between measures because the senior leadership team changed over the engagement.

Quotation. Quoted material is verbatim, with omissions marked by ellipsis. Material from interviews that were not recorded is reported in narrator prose and never placed in quotation marks. Bold emphasis is ours throughout. Individual leaders are identified by role only.

Limitations. The limits of the evidence — the absence of a second assessment round, and the constraint confidentiality placed on what the sponsors could observe — are stated in section 14 rather than in this note.

Independence. Produced by Syntoria as an independent third party. Neither party approved the selection of evidence. Full provenance records and verification grades are held in the case file.
