Your story, told well

Josh asked whether the coaching engagement you sponsored could become a written case study. This page explains what that is, what it involves for you, and what you get from it.

What this is

A case study is a short, verified account of a real coaching engagement — the situation, the decision to invest, the work, and the results — written by The Syntoria Institute and validated by you before anyone else reads it.

  • One recorded interview: 60 minutes, by video, at a date you choose.
  • Two reviews and your written validation of the final one-pager.
  • The right to stop the process at any time.
  • Nothing is shown to Josh, or to anyone else, before you validate it.
  • At the end, you set the level of confidentiality you want.
How it works

Written agreement

You give your written agreement by answering the short form below. Sébastien then confirms the practical details with you by email.

Coach's interview

Sébastien interviews Josh separately about the engagement: the context, his approach, and the work delivered.

Your interview

60 minutes, by video, recorded, at a date you choose. Sébastien conducts the interview; Josh is not present.

Writing of the one-pager

The Syntoria Institute's team writes a one-pager built from your own words. It becomes the source of truth for any further material — article, LinkedIn post, quotes.

Review and validation

Two review rounds: you change or cut anything, and nothing exists until you validate the final text in writing. You can validate every material produced, or simply the one-pager.

Publication

Josh uses the produced materials strictly within the authorizations you have given: level of privacy, channels, formats.

The method

Three rules govern every story the Institute publishes:

01
Verified accounts, not testimonials

Outcomes are documented through in-depth interviews of a real engagement — coach and client interviewed separately — then verified with the people involved.

Brinkerhoff, R. O. (2003). The Success Case Method. Berrett-Koehler.
02
An honest analysis of impact

The study shows the real impact of the coach's work through an honest, unbiased analysis: results are reported as what the coaching contributed, alongside the other factors at play.

Mayne, J. (2001). Addressing attribution through contribution analysis. Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation.
03
Evidence-labeled, validated words

Every claim in the final story is labeled by its level of evidence and quotes are used verbatim. If an account doesn't meet the Institute's standards of evidence, it is not published.
